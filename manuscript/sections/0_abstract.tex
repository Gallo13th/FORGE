Native sequence recovery is a central metric in RNA inverse folding, yet what a high score measures, and how it informs design, remains unclear. Here we introduce FORGE, an interpretable framework that maps a seven-atom RNA backbone into 935 geometric descriptors to separate training familiarity, family-level transfer and direct geometric readout. Across 4,135 post-2025 chains, recovery was 64.6\%, 87.1\% on
training-sequence repeats, 55.2\% on sequence-novel, cluster-disjoint chains and
48.9\% below 80\% identity. Separate in-domain evaluations reached
70.0\% with family overlap and 50.4\% with families held out. Beyond identity, FORGE predicts base pairing, stacking and solvent
exposure, while calibrated confidence isolates reliable residue subsets. When applied to experimental designs and solved AI-designed pseudoknots, FORGE separates backbone compatibility, AlphaFold~3 consistency and experimental outcomes. Together, these analyses provide a basis for reporting what RNA inverse-folding models recover, where they transfer and how their scores relate to experimental design evidence.
